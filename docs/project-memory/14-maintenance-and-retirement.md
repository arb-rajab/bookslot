# Maintenance and Retirement
> Purpose: the whole life of the system, including its end.
> Project: bookslot (PRIVATE track)
> Last updated: 2026-08-23 (Session 0/1 — light pass, see note)

This file's public-track counterpart in this developer's other work
(`privacy-forge`) plans for an open-source project's end-of-life and
handover to a community or successor maintainer. That framing doesn't
transfer directly to a private commercial product with real paying
customers — retirement here means either shutting down a live product
customers depend on, or a business event (acquisition, sunset), both of
which carry real obligations to real businesses that don't exist yet.
This session records the framing difference; the substantive content is
deferred until there's something real to plan around.

## What will matter once real customers exist (not yet applicable)

- **Data export and portability commitment.** If this product shuts down
  or a customer leaves, the studio owner should be able to export their
  own business's data (customers, appointment history) — this is a
  concrete commitment worth designing for from early on, not a retirement-
  day afterthought, precisely because trust with a small-business owner's
  customer data is the product's core credibility.
- **Data retention and deletion schedule**, including the GDPR-erasure-
  equivalent path noted in `06-security-threat-model.md`.
- **Support model and expectations** for paying customers (response time,
  channels) — to be defined once there's a real pricing/support
  relationship.

## Deferred entirely (not applicable yet)

- Maintenance cadence (dependency updates, patching SLA, backup-restore
  test schedule) — there is no running system to maintain.
- Handover pack, decommissioning procedure, end-of-life policy — these
  presuppose a live product with real customers depending on it, which
  doesn't exist yet.
- Dependency support horizons — no dependencies exist yet.
